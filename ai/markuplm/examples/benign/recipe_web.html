A simple vegetable and lentil soup

This recipe makes four generous bowls of soup using ordinary pantry ingredients. It is written for home cooks who want an inexpensive meal that can be prepared in one saucepan. Allow fifteen minutes for chopping and about thirty minutes for cooking. Red lentils soften quickly and give the soup a thick texture without cream. Carrots and tomatoes add sweetness, while a squeeze of lemon at the end keeps the flavour fresh. Adjust the seasoning after simmering, because the saltiness of vegetable stock varies considerably.

Ingredients for four servings

Use one onion, two carrots, two celery stalks, two cloves of garlic, one cup of red lentils, a can of chopped tomatoes, and one litre of vegetable stock. You will also need a tablespoon of olive oil, half a teaspoon of ground cumin, and a little lemon juice. Rinse the lentils in a sieve before cooking. Fresh parsley is optional; a slice of ordinary bread is a useful accompaniment.

Cooking the vegetables

Finely chop the onion, carrots, and celery. Warm the oil over medium heat and cook the vegetables for about eight minutes, stirring occasionally. They should soften rather than brown. Add the chopped garlic and cumin, then stir for another minute. If the pan becomes dry, add a spoonful of water instead of increasing the heat. This gentle beginning helps the finished soup taste rounded.

Simmering and serving

Stir in the lentils, tomatoes, and stock. Bring the mixture to a gentle simmer and cook for twenty to twenty-five minutes, until the lentils are tender. Stir from time to time so they do not stick to the bottom. Add more water if you prefer a thinner soup. Taste, season, and finish with lemon juice. Serve warm in bowls, with parsley scattered over the top if available.

Storage and variations

Cool leftovers promptly and store them in a covered container in the refrigerator. Reheat the portion you plan to eat until it is thoroughly hot, adding water if it has thickened. You can replace celery with a small leek or add a handful of spinach near the end of cooking. For a smoother texture, blend part of the soup and stir it back into the saucepan.
