Understanding Cannabidiol

Cannabidiol commonly referred to just as CBD, is a chemical compound that is produced naturally by the cannabis plant (marijuana plant). All species of cannabis contain CBD, but the Cannabis sativa is the most common plant grown for hemp and oil production. For centuries, physicians and holistic healers have turned to cannabis to treat a variety of health conditions such as pain, nausea, vomiting, anxiety, and other mood disorders. During the 1960s medical researchers started to identify the cannabinoids of the cannabis plant. So far, more than 80 individual cannabinoids have been detected. Most people are familiar with the cannabinoid tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) that has been used to attain a psychoactive high by users. However, the other cannabinoids of the plant, such as CBD, have no psychoactive properties. Interestingly, CBD is often used to counteract the psychoactive effects of THC. Scientists, medical doctors, and researchers are exploring the various health benefits of cannabinoids.
